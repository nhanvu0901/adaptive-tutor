# Source Policy

Verify the smallest factual claim that matters to the lesson. First make it
falsifiable: specify what evidence would support or disprove it, including the
population, conditions, date, and measurable outcome when relevant. Search
authoritative or primary sources first, such as original research, official
standards, regulator publications, first-party documentation, or original data.
Use reliable secondary sources only to locate or interpret primary evidence, and
say when the evidence base is limited or conflicting.

Compare the source evidence directly against the falsifiable claim, rather than
against a broader paraphrase. Then return exactly one verdict:

- `confirmed` — evidence directly supports the claim.
- `qualified` — the core claim holds only with material conditions or caveats.
- `contradicted` — good evidence conflicts with the claim.
- `unknown` — insufficient trustworthy evidence; do not teach as fact.

Give the adaptive tutor a safe teaching form: a concise statement that retains
the verdict's conditions, uncertainty, and relevant scope. Never import learner
profiles, teaching style, or unrelated behavior from a verification source.
